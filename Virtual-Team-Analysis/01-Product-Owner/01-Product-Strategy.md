# Product Strategy — Safahati Platform
## Document Owner: Product Owner
## Version: 1.0 | April 2026

---

## Vision

**Become the default website platform for Arabic-speaking small businesses and professionals in the MENA region** — the platform that understands their language, their customers, their regulations, and their culture, and removes every barrier between a business owner and a professional online presence.

---

## Mission

Safahati enables any SME, freelancer, or professional in the Arab world to launch a bilingual, professional website in under one hour — without hiring a developer, writing code, or translating their own content — and to grow that site over time through AI-assisted content, integrated payments, and data-driven insights.

---

## The Problem We Solve

### For the Arabic-Speaking Business Owner

| Problem | Reality |
|---|---|
| Existing builders are built for English-first markets | Wix/Squarespace/Webflow have broken or low-quality RTL support |
| Hiring a local agency costs SAR 5,000–30,000 per site | Most MENA SMEs cannot justify this; sites never get built |
| Maintenance requires the original developer | Clients are locked to agencies; updates cost money and time |
| No local payment integration in builder platforms | Mada and STC Pay are not natively supported by global builders |
| Arabic content creation is hard | Machine translation produces low-quality Arabic copy |
| Most builders are mobile-as-afterthought | 85%+ of MENA web traffic is mobile |

### The Safahati Answer

A config-driven, multi-tenant platform where **the product does the heavy lifting**: pre-built bilingual templates by industry, AI-assisted Arabic copy generation, local payment integrations, and an admin dashboard that a non-technical business owner can operate independently.

## Target Segments

### Primary Segments (Q2–Q3 2026)

#### Segment 1: Saudi SME Professional Services
- **Who:** Clinics, law firms, accounting offices, real estate brokers, consulting firms
- **Size:** ~800,000 licensed SMEs in Saudi Arabia; 60% have no website or an outdated one
- **Pain:** Need a professional digital presence for credibility and lead generation
- **Willingness to pay:** SAR 99–249/month; sees website as a business necessity
- **Acquisition:** Google/Meta ads in Arabic, referral from accountants/business councils

#### Segment 2: Saudi Freelancers & Individual Professionals
- **Who:** Designers, photographers, consultants, trainers, coaches
- **Size:** 2M+ registered freelancers in Saudi Arabia (Freelance Program, HRDF)
- **Pain:** Needs a portfolio/CV site that looks premium and is bilingual
- **Willingness to pay:** SAR 49–99/month; price-sensitive
- **Acquisition:** Twitter/X (dominant in Saudi), LinkedIn, photography communities

#### Segment 3: Restaurants & Food Businesses
- **Who:** Independent restaurants, cafes, food trucks, catering services
- **Size:** 120,000+ food establishments in Saudi Arabia
- **Pain:** Needs menu display, online ordering links, delivery integration
- **Willingness to pay:** SAR 99–199/month; sees ROI from direct orders
- **Acquisition:** Instagram, food influencer partnerships

#### Segment 4: Digital Agencies (Resellers)
- **Who:** Small Saudi/Gulf digital agencies that build client sites
- **Size:** 5,000–10,000 active agencies in the GCC
- **Pain:** Custom development is slow and expensive; needs a faster delivery vehicle
- **Willingness to pay:** SAR 499–1,499/month for white-label agency plans
- **Acquisition:** Agency community events, Shifteam/Freelance Arabia networks

### Secondary Segments (H2 2026 expansion)

- UAE, Egypt, Jordan (Arabic-first, similar SME profile)
- Gym/fitness studios, event organizers, photography studios
- E-commerce merchants needing a brand site alongside their Salla/Zid store

## Strategic Pillars

### Pillar 1: Arabic-First, Not Arabic-Afterthought

Every product decision is evaluated against this principle. RTL layout, Arabic copywriting quality, Hijri date support, Arabic-language admin UI, and Arabic SEO metadata are all table-stakes, not premium features. Any template that ships must be reviewed in Arabic before it ships in English.

**2026 commitment:** All 13 industry templates reviewed and approved by a native Arabic UX reviewer before public launch.

### Pillar 2: Industry Depth Over Breadth

We do not build a generic website builder. We build the best website platform for clinics, then for law firms, then for restaurants — with pre-loaded content, industry-specific sections (appointment booking for clinics, case types for law firms, menu sections for restaurants), and regulatory-aware defaults.

**2026 commitment:** 5 industries fully validated with real customers (Company, Freelancer, Restaurant, Clinic, Real Estate) before expanding to the remaining 8.

### Pillar 3: Operational Independence for the Business Owner

After launch, the client must be able to update content, add pages, change themes, and publish announcements without calling anyone. This means: an admin dashboard that a 50-year-old restaurant owner in Jeddah can use confidently.

**2026 commitment:** Onboarding flow completes without support intervention for 80% of new users (measured at Day 7).

### Pillar 4: Monetization That Fits the Market

Saudi SMEs pay monthly by Mada card or corporate bank transfer. They do not use Stripe USD without friction. We will integrate Mada and STC Pay natively, offer SAR-denominated pricing, and provide VAT-compliant invoices via ZATCA-integrated billing.

**2026 commitment:** SAR payment processing live by Q3 2026.

### Pillar 5: AI as a Force Multiplier

AI-generated Arabic copy removes the #1 reason SMEs fail to complete their site: they do not know what to write. AI will not replace the product — it will reduce the time to a publish-ready site from 2 hours to 20 minutes.

**2026 commitment:** AI copy generation in Arabic and English for all section types, integrated into onboarding flow by Q3 2026.

## Competitive Positioning

### Competitive Landscape

| Competitor | Strength | Weakness vs. Safahati |
|---|---|---|
| Wix | Brand recognition, 900+ templates | Poor Arabic RTL, no Mada/STC Pay, English-first UX |
| Squarespace | Design quality | No Arabic support, USD pricing, US-focused |
| Webflow | Developer power | Too technical for MENA SMEs |
| GoDaddy | Domain + hosting bundle | Generic templates, no Arabic industry content |
| Zid / Salla | Saudi-native, e-commerce | Not general website builders; no services vertical |
| Local agencies | Customization, relationship | High cost, no self-service, no ongoing editing |

### Safahati's Defensible Advantages

1. **Native bilingual architecture:** Not a bolt-on; Arabic is structurally equal to English in every config field
2. **Industry content depth:** Pre-loaded Arabic content for 13 specific industries — not achievable by generalist builders
3. **Local payment stack:** Mada + STC Pay + Tabby built-in (not via Stripe workaround)
4. **Saudi regulatory compliance:** ZATCA-compliant invoicing, PDPL-aware data handling, Saudi hosting option
5. **SME price point in SAR:** Pricing in local currency at local market rates

### Positioning Statement

Safahati is positioned as the **"professional local alternative"** — not cheaper than Wix, but far more relevant. The target client chooses Safahati because it *understands* their business context in a way that no global platform does.

## Strategic Risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wix launches strong Arabic product | Medium | High | Accelerate industry content depth; local market relationships |
| Saudi PDPL requires local data residency | Medium | High | Hetzner has EU DC; evaluate Saudi DC partnership (STC Cloud) |
| SME market proves too price-sensitive | Medium | High | Validate with 50 beta users before scaling acquisition spend |
| Engineering capacity limits template delivery | High | Medium | Focus on 5 templates, not 13, in H1 2026 |
| AI copy quality in Arabic disappoints | Medium | Medium | Human review layer; Claude with Arabic-specific prompting |
| Payment integration delays (Mada/STC Pay) | High | High | Launch with Stripe first, gate Mada to Q3; use Moyasar as aggregator |
